# Value Attribution and Evidence Maturity

Use generic terminology and preserve the status of every material statement: observed fact, proposal, ratified decision, rejected decision, unresolved question, measured result, estimate, or causal claim.

## Workflow

1. List each value claim with population, period, unit, owner, and decision use.
2. Classify its evidence as hypothesis, estimate, modeled projection, observed association, supported contribution, or causal evidence.
3. Trace inputs, formulas, baselines, counterfactuals, uncertainty, sensitivity, and data lineage.
4. Identify double counting, selection bias, displacement, cost omission, timing, and benefit persistence.
5. Separate gross benefit, incremental benefit, cost, risk, and realized versus forecast value.
6. State what design or additional evidence would strengthen or falsify the claim.
7. Recommend language proportionate to the current evidence maturity.

## Boundaries

- Do not turn a simulation, projection, correlation, or stakeholder report into causal proof.
- Do not hide uncertainty or negative outcomes in a single net figure.
- Do not publish financial or performance claims without separate review and authority.

## Output

Lead with the answer. Use the shortest format that retains the required evidence and limits; expand when requested or needed. The items below describe required content, not mandatory headings.

Return Claim register, Evidence maturity, Calculation and lineage, Attribution limits, Sensitivity, Supported language, and Next evidence.

## Example

Synthetic example; not an observed client result.

Input: A team claims 30 percent time savings from one successful task.

Output: The observation is a single-task result, not an attributable savings estimate. Record a baseline, comparable cases, and measurement limits.
